Focus topic

🌡️ Inflammation

What it is, how to measure it, and which peptides this tool links to it.

Two kinds

Short-term (acute) is healthy. It's the redness and swelling that repairs a cut, a strain or an infection, then switches off.

Long-term (chronic, low-grade) doesn't switch off. It's linked with joint pain, gut problems, skin flare-ups, insulin resistance and heart disease.

Where it shows up

  • 🌀Gut — bloating, IBS/IBD symptoms, food reactions
  • 🦴Joints & tendons — stiffness, swelling, slow healing
  • ✨Skin — redness, acne, eczema-type flares
  • 🔥Whole body — fatigue, aches, brain fog

Blood tests to ask about

  • 🩸hs-CRP — the main general inflammation marker
  • 🩸ESR — a slower-moving inflammation marker
  • 🩸CBC with differential — white blood cell picture
  • 🩸HbA1c / fasting insulin — blood sugar, a big driver of chronic inflammation

Test before and during any research so you can see whether anything actually changes.

The basics move the numbers most: sleep, a healthy waistline, regular movement, fewer ultra-processed foods and enough omega-3. Peptides are researched as an add-on, not a replacement.

🩸 Suggested Blood Work BEFORE Starting Any Protocol
Inform your doctor. Protect your health. You are the most important member of your healthcare team — be honest and detailed about everything you're taking or planning to take. Sharing the full picture helps your doctor catch issues early and build a plan that fits your needs.

📋 Comprehensive Metabolic & Hormone Panel: Comprehensive Metabolic Panel (CMP) · Complete Blood Count (CBC) · Lipid Panel (Total Cholesterol, LDL, HDL, Triglycerides, VLDL) · hs-CRP · ESR · TSH · Free T4 · Estradiol (Sensitive) · Total Testosterone · Free Testosterone · SHBG · LH · FSH · Prolactin · DHEA-S · Cortisol (AM) · Vitamin D (25-OH) · Vitamin B12 · Folate · Iron Panel · Ferritin · Magnesium · Zinc · Liver Enzymes (ALT, AST, GGT) · Kidney Function (BUN, Creatinine)


🦋 Ask Your Doctor to Add — Thyroid Panel: Thyroid issues (hypo/hyperthyroidism) can mimic or worsen symptoms people often blame on peptides — fatigue, weight changes, mood, heart rate. If not already included, ask for: TSH, Free T3, Free T4, and Thyroid Antibodies (TPO/TgAb) if there's a family history of thyroid disease.


🍬 Ask Your Doctor to Add — Insulin Resistance Panel: Insulin resistance can drive fat gain, cravings, and energy crashes — and is often missed on a standard panel since fasting glucose alone can look "normal." If not already included, ask for: Fasting Insulin, Fasting Glucose, HbA1c (3-month average blood sugar), and HOMA-IR (calculated from fasting glucose + insulin).


🔥 Ask Your Doctor to Add — Inflammation Panel: Chronic inflammation can affect recovery, gut health, joints, and how well your body responds to peptides. If not already included, ask for: hs-CRP (high-sensitivity C-Reactive Protein), ESR (Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate), and Homocysteine.


🟢 Ask Your Doctor to Add — Liver Panel: Many peptides (and underlying issues like fatty liver) are processed through the liver, so a baseline liver panel helps your doctor catch problems early and track changes over time. If not already included, ask for: ALT, AST, ALP (Alkaline Phosphatase), GGT, Total & Direct Bilirubin, Albumin, and Total Protein.


Even if your doctor disagrees, talk to them anyway. They may catch risks, suggest safer alternatives, or adjust their perspective as new research emerges. Always be honest with your doctor — if they refuse to help or listen, find another. You deserve a doctor who supports you.

VIP — 50–100mcg SubQ 2–3x/week · one of the most potent endogenous anti-inflammatory neuropeptides; suppresses TNF-α, IL-6, IL-12. Studied in RA, IBD, and lupus. 📖 Delgado et al. 2004, PMID 14978027
Thymosin Alpha-1 — 1.6mg 2x/week SubQ · restores Treg/Th17 balance; regulatory T-cell deficiency allows autoimmune overactivation to persist. Used clinically for autoimmune hepatitis and lupus. 📖 Tuthill et al. 2006, PMID 16336577
GHK-Cu — 1–2mg/day SubQ · reduces IL-6 and TNF-α while stimulating collagen synthesis. 📖 Pickart et al. 2015, PMID 26060882
SS-31 (Elamipretide) — 1–2mg/day SubQ · improves mitochondrial function and reduces ROS — mitochondrial dysfunction amplifies chronic autoimmune inflammation and fatigue. 📖 Szeto 2014, PMID 25251836
Thymulin — 0.05–0.1mg SubQ 2–3x/week · zinc-dependent thymus hormone that corrects T-regulatory deficits; stack with Zinc. 📖 Dardenne 2002, PMID 11812338
MOTS-c — 5–10mg 2–3x/week SubQ · activates AMPK which directly suppresses NF-κB and reduces pro-inflammatory cytokine output. 📖 Lee et al. 2015, PMID 25738459
⚠ GLP-1 NOTE: Semaglutide, Tirzepatide, and Retatrutide suppress the same cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-17) targeted by expensive biologic drugs. If you're on a GLP-1 and have an autoimmune condition, discuss the immune implications with your rheumatologist.
🦋 Hashimoto's & Lupus — Vitamins, Supplements & Peptides
Hashimoto's and lupus are both autoimmune, but they're not the same fight: Hashimoto's is the immune system attacking the thyroid specifically, while lupus (SLE) is systemic — it can hit joints, skin, kidneys, and other organs. Listed separately below because the supporting research and peptide targets differ.
🦋 HASHIMOTO'S THYROIDITIS
💊 SUPPLEMENTS & VITAMINS
Selenium (Selenomethionine) — 200mcg/day · consistently shown in meta-analyses (e.g. Wichman et al., Thyroid, 2016) to reduce TPO-antibody levels; the thyroid has the highest selenium concentration of any organ and needs it for antioxidant enzyme function.
Myo-Inositol + Selenium (combo) — 600mg myo-inositol + 83mcg selenium 2x/day · studied specifically in Hashimoto's for lowering TSH and antibody titers when used together, more than selenium alone.
Vitamin D3 + K2 — 5,000–10,000 IU D3 + 100mcg K2 daily · VDR activation on T-cells modulates immune activity; deficiency is near-universal in Hashimoto's. 📖 Correale et al. 2009, PMID 19604026
Zinc + Copper (balanced) — 25–30mg zinc / 1–2mg copper daily · both are cofactors for thyroid hormone conversion (T4→T3) and immune regulation.
Low-Dose Naltrexone (LDN) — 1.5–4.5mg/night · off-label for Hashimoto's; modulates TLR4/immune signaling. 📖 Younger & Mackey 2009, PMID 19265012
🧬 PEPTIDES DISCUSSED FOR HASHIMOTO'S
Thymulin — 0.05–0.1mg SubQ 2–3x/week (zinc-dependent) · supports T-regulatory cell function relevant to thyroid autoimmunity; very low "microdose" protocols (e.g. 250mcg) are discussed in community research as gentler starting points.
VIP — very low "microdose" (e.g. 250mcg) SubQ · immunoregulatory; discussed for calming overactive thyroid-directed immune response.
GHK-Cu — 1–2mg/day SubQ · anti-inflammatory, also the basis of the AHK-Cu hair-regrowth protocol used for autoimmune-related hair thinning (see Cheat Sheet Vault).
🔴 LUPUS (SLE)
💊 SUPPLEMENTS & VITAMINS
Vitamin D3 + K2 — 5,000–10,000 IU D3 + 100mcg K2 daily · deficiency independently predicts lupus flares. 📖 Correale et al. 2009, PMID 19604026
Omega-3 (EPA + DHA) — 3–4g/day · reduces inflammatory eicosanoids relevant to lupus joint and skin flares.
NAC (N-Acetyl Cysteine) — 600mg 2–3x/day · precursor to glutathione; studied specifically in SLE for reducing disease activity via T-cell mTOR modulation. 📖 Lai et al. 2010, PMID 20048489
L-Carnitine — dosed for cardiovascular/energy support · supports mitochondrial energy production and endothelial function, relevant given the high cardiovascular risk in SLE.
🧬 PEPTIDES DISCUSSED FOR LUPUS
P140 (Lupuzor) — investigational, research-use only · the single most lupus-specific peptide in the literature. Derived from the U1-70K spliceosomal protein; blocks overactive chaperone-mediated autophagy, lowers MHC class II expression on B cells, and reduces autoreactive lymphocyte activity — restoring immune tolerance rather than broadly suppressing immunity. Reached late-stage clinical trials (ImmuPharma).
VIP & Thymulin — very low "microdose" protocols (e.g. 250mcg) discussed in the literature for immune regulation in SLE; may take longer to show effect at these doses.
⚠ LL-37 CAUTION: This cathelicidin peptide is sometimes discussed for other purposes, but it can form complexes with self-DNA and may activate TLR9/type I interferon pathways — mechanisms directly implicated in lupus flares. Human evidence is mixed, but the theoretical risk in lupus specifically means this is one to approach with real caution, not one to reach for by default.
⚠ GLP-1 NOTE: Semaglutide, tirzepatide, and retatrutide's anti-inflammatory effects (↓TNF-α, ↓IL-6, ↓IL-17, ↑Tregs) extend to lupus in early case reports and preclinical models — formal trials are still underway, discuss with your rheumatologist before combining.

🎗️ Cancer Support & Chemo Recovery — Immune Rebuilding, Thymosin Alpha-1 & Black Seed Oil
⚠ This section is for research and educational purposes only. Nothing here is medical advice or a substitute for oncology care. Always discuss everything with your oncologist before adding any supplement or peptide during cancer treatment.
💉 THYMOSIN ALPHA-1 — IMMUNE REBUILDER DURING CHEMO / INFUSIONS
Thymosin Alpha-1 (TA-1) is a thymic peptide that rebuilds the immune system's ability to fight cancer while conventional treatment is happening. Chemotherapy suppresses white blood cell production — TA-1 directly counteracts this by restoring T-cell maturation, NK (natural killer) cell activity, and dendritic cell function. It has been used clinically in Italy as adjunct cancer immunotherapy, studied in breast cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma, and non-small cell lung cancer.

Key mechanisms during chemotherapy:
• Rebuilds T-cell and NK cell populations suppressed by chemo
• Enhances tumor antigen recognition (dendritic cell activation)
• Reduces treatment-related infections by restoring innate immunity
• Anti-tumor activity: stimulates IL-2, IFN-γ, TNF-α against cancer cells
• Shown to improve overall survival as adjunct therapy in some cancer trials

Research protocol (discuss with oncologist): 1.6mg SubQ 2x/week during chemo cycles.
📖 Garaci et al. 2012, PMID 22325148 · Lo et al. 2022 (NSCLC + TA-1)
🌿 BLACK SEED OIL (NIGELLA SATIVA) — DURING & POST CHEMO
Black Seed Oil (Nigella sativa) — primarily via its active compound Thymoquinone (TQ) — has been studied extensively for both anti-cancer and chemo-protective effects.

During chemotherapy — protective effects:
• Reduces cisplatin-induced kidney and liver toxicity
• Protects against doxorubicin cardiotoxicity (heart protection)
• Reduces methotrexate-induced oxidative damage via glutathione upregulation
• Reduces myelosuppression (bone marrow suppression) from chemo in some models
• Anti-nausea and appetite support effects documented

Anti-tumor mechanisms studied:
• Induces apoptosis via p53 and caspase-3 activation
• Inhibits NF-κB — blocks tumor-promoting inflammation
• Studied in breast, lung, colon, cervical, liver, and blood cancers

Post-chemo recovery:
• Liver-protective — supports recovery of liver detox capacity
• Immune-modulating — helps restore NK and T-cell activity
• Gut-protective — supports intestinal recovery after chemo

Dose (research context): 1–2 tsp cold-pressed oil daily, or 500–1,000mg capsule form, with food.
📖 Badary et al. 2000, PMID 10822163 · Gali-Muhtasib et al. 2006, PMID 16537386 · Ahmad et al. 2013, PMID 23823094
💊 OTHER SUPPLEMENTS FOR CHEMO SUPPORT
Glutathione (IV or Liposomal) — 600–1,200mg IV 2–3x/week OR 500–1,000mg liposomal daily · protects healthy cells, reduces neurotoxicity of platinum-based chemo. 📖 Smyth et al. 1997, PMID 9063052
NAC (N-Acetyl Cysteine) — 600–1,200mg/day · precursor to glutathione; reduces chemo-induced oxidative damage and mucositis. 📖 Amores-Sánchez & Medina 1999, PMID 10570349
Vitamin D3 — 5,000–10,000 IU/day · low D3 independently associated with worse cancer outcomes; upregulates cancer-suppressing genes. 📖 Garland et al. 2009, PMID 19406338

Magnesium Glycinate — 400mg/day · chemo (especially cisplatin) causes severe magnesium wasting; repletion reduces neurotoxicity. 📖 Lajer et al. 1996, PMID 8869528
🧬 PEPTIDES FOR CANCER SUPPORT

🧪 Reconstitution Info & Example (mg, mcg, units)
Easy rule: 1 mg = 1000 mcg. This calculator assumes a 100-unit (1mL) insulin syringe, where each unit = 0.01 mL.

MG to MCG quick chart: 0.025mg = 25mcg · 0.05mg = 50mcg · 0.1mg = 100mcg · 0.25mg = 250mcg · 0.3mg = 300mcg · 0.33mg = 330mcg · 0.5mg = 500mcg · 1mg = 1000mcg.

Example: You add 3 mL of BAC water to a 20mg vial, and your desired dose is 0.5mg (500mcg).
Draw amount = (desired dose ÷ total vial mg) × (mL added × 100 units/mL) = (0.5 ÷ 20) × 300 = 7.5 units (0.075 mL on a 100u syringe).

✨ GHK-Cu — What It's For & How to Use It
GHK-Cu (Copper Peptide) is a copper-binding peptide studied for skin repair (collagen/elastin production), wound healing, hair growth, anti-inflammatory effects, and tissue remodeling. It's commonly suggested for Skin & Hair, Joints, Recovery, Anti-Aging, and Inflammation goals above.

Typical use: Dosed once daily, commonly around 1-2mg/day, for 8-12 week cycles. Some research users apply it topically as well as via injection.

Note: Copper peptides can oxidize/discolor in solution over time — store reconstituted vials in the fridge and watch for color changes (a slight blue/green tint is normal, but a strong shift may mean it's degrading).
