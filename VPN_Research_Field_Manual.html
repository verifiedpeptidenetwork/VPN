TALK TO YOUR DOCTOR — BEFORE YOU RESEARCH ANYTHING

Before researching any peptide referenced in this manual, speak with your physician. Tell them exactly what you are researching and considering. Full disclosure means your doctor knows what is in your system, what to look for, and how to respond quickly and correctly if something goes wrong. Going in quiet helps no one — least of all you.

IF YOUR DOCTOR WON'T ENGAGE

If your doctor refuses to have this conversation with you, will not work with you, or dismisses your questions outright — fire them and find one who will. Your safety depends on having a physician who is informed and prepared, not one who looks away.

BLOOD WORK & LAB GUIDE

Baseline labs belong before any research protocol — not after something feels off. The four panels below cover the systems most commonly affected. Each panel includes a one-tap copy button formatted to drop straight into an email to your doctor.

BLOOD WORK & LAB GUIDE·PANEL 1 OF 4
🧾

INSULIN RESISTANCE PANEL

Tracks how your body is handling glucose and insulin — important when researching compounds that affect appetite, fat storage, or metabolic rate.

> Fasting Insulin
> Fasting Glucose
> HbA1c
> HOMA-IR
BLOOD WORK & LAB GUIDE·PANEL 2 OF 4
🔥

INFLAMMATION PANEL

Establishes a baseline inflammatory marker level before starting any research protocol, and flags adverse reactions early if levels move.

> hs-CRP (high-sensitivity C-Reactive Protein)
> ESR (Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate)
> Homocysteine
BLOOD WORK & LAB GUIDE·PANEL 3 OF 4
🧬

LIVER PANEL

Most research compounds are processed through the liver. Routine liver panels catch problems early, long before symptoms appear.

> ALT (Alanine Aminotransferase)
> AST (Aspartate Aminotransferase)
> ALP (Alkaline Phosphatase)
> GGT (Gamma-Glutamyl Transferase)
> Total & Direct Bilirubin
> Albumin
> Total Protein

METABOLIC PANEL

A broad systemic check of kidney function, electrolytes, and lipids — the background context every other panel in this guide should be read against.

> Glucose
> BUN (Blood Urea Nitrogen)
> Creatinine & eGFR
> Sodium, Potassium, Chloride, CO2
> Calcium
> Lipid Panel (Total Cholesterol, LDL, HDL, Triglycerides)
> Uric Acid

APPENDIX A — RECONSTITUTION & STORAGE REFERENCE

DILUENT VEHICLES

Bacteriostatic water (BAC water) is the standard diluent referenced throughout this manual. It contains 0.9% benzyl alcohol as a preservative, which extends usable life after reconstitution compared to sterile water alone.

STABILITY FACTORS

  • Store reconstituted vials refrigerated (2–8°C / 36–46°F)
  • Avoid agitation — swirl gently, never shake
  • Protect from direct light
  • Discard per the source sheet's stated cycle window once opened

STERILITY FACTORS

  • Use a new sterile syringe/needle for every draw
  • Wipe vial tops with alcohol before each puncture
  • Never reintroduce used needles into a shared vial

DATA STATUS

Reconstitution guidance above is general handling information, not a substitute for the specific instructions on file with a sourced product. When in doubt, default to the most conservative storage and handling practice.
