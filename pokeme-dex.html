🩸 Suggested Blood Work BEFORE Starting Any Protocol
Inform your doctor. Protect your health. You are the most important member of your healthcare team — be honest and detailed about everything you're taking or planning to take. Sharing the full picture helps your doctor catch issues early and build a plan that fits your needs.

📋 Comprehensive Metabolic & Hormone Panel: Comprehensive Metabolic Panel (CMP) · Complete Blood Count (CBC) · Lipid Panel (Total Cholesterol, LDL, HDL, Triglycerides, VLDL) · hs-CRP · ESR · TSH · Free T4 · Estradiol (Sensitive) · Total Testosterone · Free Testosterone · SHBG · LH · FSH · Prolactin · DHEA-S · Cortisol (AM) · Vitamin D (25-OH) · Vitamin B12 · Folate · Iron Panel · Ferritin · Magnesium · Zinc · Liver Enzymes (ALT, AST, GGT) · Kidney Function (BUN, Creatinine)


🦋 Ask Your Doctor to Add — Thyroid Panel: Thyroid issues (hypo/hyperthyroidism) can mimic or worsen symptoms people often blame on peptides — fatigue, weight changes, mood, heart rate. If not already included, ask for: TSH, Free T3, Free T4, and Thyroid Antibodies (TPO/TgAb) if there's a family history of thyroid disease.


🍬 Ask Your Doctor to Add — Insulin Resistance Panel: Insulin resistance can drive fat gain, cravings, and energy crashes — and is often missed on a standard panel since fasting glucose alone can look "normal." If not already included, ask for: Fasting Insulin, Fasting Glucose, HbA1c (3-month average blood sugar), and HOMA-IR (calculated from fasting glucose + insulin).


🔥 Ask Your Doctor to Add — Inflammation Panel: Chronic inflammation can affect recovery, gut health, joints, and how well your body responds to peptides. If not already included, ask for: hs-CRP (high-sensitivity C-Reactive Protein), ESR (Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate), and Homocysteine.


🟢 Ask Your Doctor to Add — Liver Panel: Many peptides (and underlying issues like fatty liver) are processed through the liver, so a baseline liver panel helps your doctor catch problems early and track changes over time. If not already included, ask for: ALT, AST, ALP (Alkaline Phosphatase), GGT, Total & Direct Bilirubin, Albumin, and Total Protein.


Even if your doctor disagrees, talk to them anyway. They may catch risks, suggest safer alternatives, or adjust their perspective as new research emerges. Always be honest with your doctor — if they refuse to help or listen, find another. You deserve a doctor who supports you.

Your health. Your responsibility. You decide.

💊 SUPPLEMENTS & VITAMINS
Omega-3 (EPA + DHA) — 3–4g/day · suppresses leukotriene B4 and PGE2; multiple RCTs show reduction in morning stiffness and joint pain in RA. 📖 Proudman et al. 2015, PMID 25320462 · Calder 2015, PMID 25800563
Vitamin D3 + K2 — 5,000–10,000 IU D3 + 100mcg K2 daily · VDR activation on T-cells and macrophages directly modulates immune activity; D deficiency is near-universal in autoimmune disease and independently predicts flares. 📖 Correale et al. 2009, PMID 19604026 · Cantorna 2010, PMID 20966408
Magnesium Glycinate — 400–600mg/day · cofactor in 300+ enzymatic reactions; magnesium deficiency amplifies NF-κB signaling and cytokine release. 📖 Mazur et al. 2007, PMID 17675186
Curcumin (with Piperine) — 500–1,000mg 2x/day · inhibits NF-κB and COX-2; RCTs show equivalent NSAID efficacy for joint pain in RA with superior GI tolerability. 📖 Chandran & Goel 2012, PMID 22407780 · Daily et al. 2016, PMID 27213821
Boswellia Serrata (AKBA) — 100–200mg AKBA/day · inhibits 5-LOX (the leukotriene pathway), reducing joint inflammation without NSAID side effects. 📖 Sengupta et al. 2010, PMID 20359267
NAC (N-Acetyl Cysteine) — 600mg 2–3x/day · precursor to glutathione; reduces oxidative stress that drives autoimmune tissue damage and cytokine storm. 📖 Lai et al. 2010, PMID 20048489
Quercetin — 500–1,000mg/day · inhibits histamine release and pro-inflammatory cytokine production; stabilizes mast cells relevant to lupus and Sjögren's. 📖 Li et al. 2016, PMID 26616308
Zinc — 25–30mg elemental/day · required for regulatory T-cell function and thymulin production; deficiency worsens autoimmune activity. 📖 Haase & Rink 2009, PMID 19371543
Probiotics (multi-strain) — 50B+ CFU/day · gut dysbiosis is a root driver of autoimmune disease; Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium strains reduce Th17/Treg imbalance central to RA, IBD, and psoriasis. 📖 Zamani et al. 2016, PMID 27370920
Low-Dose Naltrexone (LDN) — 1.5–4.5mg/night · modulates TLR4 signaling and microglial activation; used off-label for RA, Crohn's, MS, psoriasis with growing evidence base. 📖 Younger & Mackey 2009, PMID 19265012 · Cree et al. 2010, PMID 20085681
🧬 PEPTIDES FOR AUTOIMMUNE & RHEUMATIC DISEASE
BPC-157 — 250–500mcg/day SubQ or oral · upregulates NO-cGMP signaling which reduces systemic immune overactivation; heals gut lining (a root trigger of autoimmune disease via leaky gut → molecular mimicry). 📖 Sikiric et al. 2018, PMID 30197782
TB-500 — 2–5mg 2x/week SubQ (loading), then 2mg/week · reduces TNF-α in inflamed connective tissue; promotes repair of cartilage, synovium, and tendons damaged by chronic inflammation in RA and psoriatic arthritis. 📖 Goldstein et al. 2012, PMID 22226572
KPV — 250–500mcg/day SubQ or oral · a direct NF-κB inhibitor via the melanocortin-1 receptor; suppresses the master inflammatory switch driving IBD, RA, and psoriasis at the cellular level. 📖 Dalmasso et al. 2008, PMID 18510698 · Kannengiesser et al. 2008
VIP (Vasoactive Intestinal Peptide) — 50–100mcg SubQ 2–3x/week · one of the most potent endogenous anti-inflammatory neuropeptides; directly suppresses TNF-α, IL-6, IL-12 in macrophages and T-cells. Studied in RA, IBD, and lupus clinical models. 📖 Delgado et al. 2004, PMID 14978027 · Abad et al. 2010, PMID 20498071
Thymosin Alpha-1 (TA-1) — 1.6mg SubQ 2x/week · restores Treg/Th17 balance — regulatory T-cell deficiency allows autoimmune overactivation to persist; TA-1 directly corrects this imbalance. Used clinically for autoimmune hepatitis and lupus in some countries. 📖 Tuthill et al. 2006, PMID 16336577 · Camerini et al. 2000
GHK-Cu — 1–2mg/day SubQ · reduces IL-6 and TNF-α while stimulating collagen synthesis — targets both the inflammatory cytokine cascade and the connective tissue destruction central to RA, psoriasis, and Sjögren's. 📖 Pickart et al. 2015, PMID 26060882
Selank — 250–500mcg/day SubQ or intranasal · modulates HPA axis and reduces stress-induced immune dysregulation; chronic HPA activation worsens autoimmune flares through cortisol-driven cytokine imbalance. 📖 Semenova et al. 2010, PMID 20803000
SS-31 (Elamipretide) — 1–2mg/day SubQ · improves mitochondrial function and reduces ROS — mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress are primary amplifiers of chronic autoimmune inflammation and fatigue. 📖 Szeto 2014, PMID 25251836
MOTS-c — 5–10mg 2–3x/week SubQ · activates AMPK which directly suppresses NF-κB and reduces pro-inflammatory cytokine output; studied for systemic anti-inflammatory and metabolic effects in immune-driven conditions. 📖 Lee et al. 2015, PMID 25738459
LL-37 — 0.5–1mg SubQ 2–3x/week · modulates innate immune signaling; reduces pathological TLR activation that drives psoriasis, IBD, and lupus while preserving antimicrobial defense. 📖 Kahlenberg & Kaplan 2013, PMID 23702143
Thymulin — 0.05–0.1mg SubQ 2–3x/week · thymus hormone that regulates T-cell maturation; zinc-dependent — supplement zinc alongside it. Corrects the T-regulatory deficits that allow autoimmune overactivation. 📖 Dardenne 2002, PMID 11812338
⚠ GLP-1 NOTE: Semaglutide, Tirzepatide, and Retatrutide suppress the same cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-17) targeted by expensive biologic drugs. If you are on a GLP-1 for weight loss AND have an autoimmune condition, discuss the immune implications with your rheumatologist — you may already be getting meaningful anti-inflammatory benefit.
